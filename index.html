Pengcheng Zhou

I am an Assistant Researcher at the South China Sea Institute of Oceanology, Chinese Academy of Sciences (SCSIO, CAS), in the Laboratory of Marginal Sea and Oceanic Geology. My research focuses on oceanic transform fault seismicity, slow-spreading mid-ocean ridge tectonics, and seismic hazard along subduction zones.

I received my Ph.D. in Earth and Atmospheric Sciences from The Chinese University of Hong Kong (2016–2021), during which I visited Stanford University (Seismology, 2019–2020). I hold an M.S. in Solid Earth Geophysics from Peking University and a B.S. in Exploration Technology and Engineering from Xi'an Shiyou University.

Research Topics

NSFC Youth Fund · 2024–2026

Seismicity and Brittle Deformation of Oceanic Transform Faults

Case study: Blanco Transform Fault System, NE Pacific

Question: What controls along-strike segmentation into seismic and aseismic slip patches?

Approach: OBS microseismicity catalog, focal mechanism inversion, Coulomb stress modeling.

Key insight: Elevated pore-fluid pressure within barrier segments promotes reverse and oblique-reverse faulting, making focal mechanism diversity a direct proxy for fault mechanical state.

SWIR Expedition · Dec 2025

Microseismicity and Crustal Structure along the Ultra-Slow Southwest Indian Ridge

Study area: 67.5°E–68.5°E, eastern SWIR

Question: How do magmatic and tectonic processes partition across a single 30 km ridge segment at <16 mm/yr spreading?

Approach: 14-OBS array; PickBlue ML phase picking; NonLinLoc + HypoDD relocation; SKHASH focal mechanisms.

Key insight: A 10–20 km along-axis transition encodes four simultaneous structural changes invisible to teleseismic catalogs.

Paper (in prep)
NSFC General · 2026–2029

Deep Flexural Deformation of the Subducting Mariana Southern Plate

Project No. 42576063 — collaborator

Question: How does outer-rise bending stress modulate shallow seismicity and regional stress state in the Mariana system?

Approach: Seismicity relocation, stress inversion, finite-element flexure modeling.

Relevance: Links plate bending, intraslab hydration, and seismic hazard in the Western Pacific.

Guangdong NSF · 2023–2025

Machine-Learning Characterization of Hydrothermal Seismicity

Case study: Loki's Castle hydrothermal vent field

Question: Can deep-learning phase pickers (PickBlue) generalize from OBS training sets to hydrothermal noise environments?

Approach: Transfer learning on OBS waveforms, template matching, seismicity migration analysis.

Relevance: Opens ML seismology to non-standard, high-noise seafloor settings.
